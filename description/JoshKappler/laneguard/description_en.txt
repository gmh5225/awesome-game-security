LaneGuard is a behavioral anti-cheat test bench built around an original simulation of a lane-change driving game for real-money mobile skill games. It implements a ladder of attacker models from naive scripted bots to stealth camouflage bots that mimic human motor noise, reaction times, and imperfect play, paired with a client-side detector that extracts kinematic and behavioral texture signals with ROC calibration. The project is written in TypeScript with a Next.js dashboard, a deterministic physics engine with SAT collision, and headless batch runners for measuring detection rates and economic break-even analysis. It demonstrates that competent stealth bots can evade client-side motor forensics while economic constraints such as rake, win rates, and population statistics provide stronger server-side binding. It is aimed at game security researchers and anti-cheat engineers evaluating the limits of behavioral detection for skill-based wagering games.
