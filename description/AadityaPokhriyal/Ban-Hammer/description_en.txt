Ban-Hammer is a machine learning pipeline that detects cheating in Counter-Strike 2 by analyzing per-player behavioral and combat statistics from match telemetry. Implemented primarily in Python with Jupyter notebooks and a feature-extraction script, it builds labeled datasets from Parquet tick data and companion JSON event logs, including cheater ground truth and derived signals such as kills and deaths per round, K/D ratio, headshot rate, movement speed, accuracy penalty, damage per round, and contextual kill ratios through smoke, while blinded, or in air. Training uses scikit-learn pipelines with logistic regression, random forest, and XGBoost classifiers, group-aware validation, and threshold tuning, with serialized models saved for evaluation on held-out matches. It targets game security researchers and anti-cheat practitioners who want a reproducible, statistics-driven approach to flagging suspicious aimbot-like or spinbot-like play from in-game data rather than client-side hooks alone.
