This repository documents a structured feasibility study of obtaining temporary root on an OPPO K3 handset with a locked bootloader, shell-only ADB access, and an Android 11 Linux 4.9.227 kernel. The work systematically evaluates five kernel and userspace attack surfaces—including KGSL, GhostLock-style futex/rtmutex races, binder CVEs, and FastRPC—and records why each path fails at a specific, verifiable gap rather than offering a working exploit. It ships primarily in Python and C: offline analyzers for vmlinux symbols, syscall stack geometry, and 4.9 structure layouts, plus on-device probe and race programs, with extensive handover notes, ADB artifacts, and kernel source references. The intended audience is mobile security researchers, reverse engineers, and kernel exploit analysts who need a reproducible negative-result case study and tooling for locked-vendor Android hardening and privilege-escalation research.
