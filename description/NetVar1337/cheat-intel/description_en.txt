cheat-intel is an open knowledge base and reporting practice for monitoring public cheat communities and turning what they reveal into actionable game-security intelligence. The repository centers on Markdown methodology, collector runbooks, and periodic landscape reports rather than shipping runtime software, though it prescribes structured record keeping such as dated JSONL or SQLite captures. It defines how to observe forums, listings, patch notes, and official anti-cheat communications; grade claims from primary artefacts through corroboration levels; and translate trends into concrete detection and process actions with confidence and falsifiers. Emphasis is on lawful OSINT only, attribution hygiene, and the intel-to-detection feedback loop covering loaders, spoofers, DMA and peripheral cheats, subscription delivery, and account markets. It is aimed at anti-cheat engineers, threat researchers, and enforcement teams who need to keep pace with cheat-market tempo without relying on rumor or unverified forum chatter.
